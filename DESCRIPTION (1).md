# About This Skill

Most audience personas are fiction. Not deliberately — nobody sits down to write something useless. But the typical process produces a document that's confident where it should be uncertain, vague where it should be specific, and built entirely from assumptions that never get tested.

You end up with a character. Age range. Location. Three "pain points" that sound like they came from a marketing textbook. A two-line before/after that says nothing concrete about anyone real. You file it away, write what you were going to write anyway, and wonder why the open rates are flat.

This skill was built to fix that.

---

## Where It Came From

The TABARC-Gaming Substack covers tabletop gaming — board games, wargames, CCGs, RPGs — for an audience that ranges from total beginners to tournament-level players. Working out who exactly we're talking to, at what point in their journey, with what expectations and frustrations and wallet sensitivity, turned out to matter enormously. Not just for newsletter content. For everything: offers, pricing, product decisions, how we position Games Haven itself.

The existing tools for this weren't good enough. Most persona frameworks ask the right questions but don't tell you what to do with the answers. You get a document. Not a decision-making tool.

So we built one.

---

## What Makes It Different

Three things.

The first is the layered structure. Not every task needs the full psychological depth of a buying profile. Content decisions need different questions than pricing decisions. The persona framework is organised into three layers so you pull what's relevant for the task and nothing else. Less friction. More use.

The second is the application playbook. For every domain — content, email subject lines, offers, pricing, product decisions, marketing copy — the skill has a specific protocol. What to invoke from the persona, what to produce, and how to check the output against the reader before you ship it. The persona isn't a reference document you look at occasionally. It's active.

The third is the Kaizen loop. The persona versions. Every application generates a brief note. Quarterly, you run a full review: pull signals, check assumptions, update what changed, archive what changed from, set new open questions. Old versions are dated and kept — not deleted — because drift matters. You want to see how your understanding of the audience has changed, and why.

The verbatim language rule is probably the most useful thing in the whole skill. When a real subscriber uses specific words — in a reply, a comment, a DM — those words go straight into the persona, unedited. Their sentence, not a summary of their sentence. That language is the source for subject lines, offer copy, and positioning. It's the difference between writing for the audience and writing as the audience.

## Who It's For

If you run a newsletter and you want content decisions to be grounded in something more reliable than "I think this is interesting," this skill will help.

If you're building offers or setting prices and you'd rather have a framework than a gut feeling, this skill will help.

If you've tried audience research before and found the output sat in a folder unused, this is built specifically to avoid that.

It was built for TABARC-Gaming and the tabletop gaming context. But the methodology is general. The questions work for any B2C newsletter — possibly for B2B too, with minor adaptation.

---

## What It Won't Do

It won't replace talking to your actual audience. No framework does. The verbatim language rule exists precisely because the best content always comes from what readers actually say, not what we imagine they'd say.

It also won't build the persona for you without input. You know things about your audience that can't be inferred from a question list. The skill is a structure for capturing and applying that knowledge — not a substitute for having it.
